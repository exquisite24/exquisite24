Getting Past the Gatekeeper

One Wealth Map RIA outreach
Each situation has a goal, a sample response and the reason behind it. Use the samples as a guide. You can adjust the wording so it sounds natural, but keep every fact and claim exactly as approved.

Know your goal

With a gatekeeper: get connected. With the owner: book the meeting.

Leave with something

Try to end each call with a transfer, a callback time, a direct line, or an email.

Keep it short

Your answer to a gatekeeper should be one or two sentences. Long answers sound like a sales call.

START HERE

Think of the gatekeeper as a building guard

A guard lets people in when they give a name and a clear reason. When someone starts explaining products, the guard usually stops them at the lobby. Gatekeepers work the same way.

A simple pattern to follow:

ShortName and company only
→
PurposeSimple reason, no pitch
→
RedirectAsk for the owner again
→
PolitePlease and thank you

Why end with a question: asking "Are they available now?" keeps the call moving toward your goal. It gives the gatekeeper something simple to answer.

Always be honest

Be confident, but only say what's true. Use your real name, the same way, on every call and follow-up. Don't say it's a follow-up call if it isn't. Don't say our founder asked for the call, came across their practice, or wants to meet them personally. Don't pretend to know the owner. Only use approved firm facts: a multi-family office, around $300 million, growing about 40% a year, and a founder who has built and sold a few firms. These calls can be recorded, and the firms we call are regulated. Honesty protects you, the client and One Wealth Map.

Before and after every call

Before you dial: check GHL and the lead source. Know if it's a firm owner or an individual advisor. Ask for the exact name in the GHL record.
Never say to a gatekeeper: anything about selling, buying, merging or recruiting, any numbers, or who else we're talking to. Owner conversations are confidential.
If someone asks to be removed: mark Do Not Call in GHL right away, before your next dial.
In the samples: [Owner] is the person you're trying to reach. Use their first name.

WORDS

With gatekeepers: words that help, words to avoid

These help

  • "Business matter" is clear and true
  • "One Wealth Map" plus your real name says clearly who is calling
  • The owner's first name shows you did your research
  • "Please" and "thank you" keep it respectful
  • "Would later today be better?" gives you a next step

Avoid these

  • "Opportunity" or "potential" sound like a sales call
  • "Offer," "services," "AI," "grow faster" also sound like a sales call
  • A long company description (location, type of firm) when they didn't ask
  • "Sorry to bother you" at the start makes the call sound unimportant
  • Trailing off mid-sentence makes it harder to finish your point
  • "Our founder asked me to call," "came across your practice," or "wants to meet you personally" are not approved
  • "More leads than we can handle" or any figure that is not approved
A

Screening questions

The gatekeeper is checking who you are and why you're calling.

#SituationGoalWhy it works
1"What's this regarding?"One short, true reason. Then ask for the owner again.Like telling a guard "I'm here to see Mr. Reyes." You don't show them your presentation. Short answers sound clear and professional.
Sample: "It's regarding a business matter for [Owner]. Are they available now?"
2"Is this a sales call?"Answer honestly: no. Then redirect to being connected.Like a guard asking "Delivery?" "No" alone can sound unclear. "No, it's a business matter" sounds like a visitor.
Sample: "No, it isn't. It's a business matter for [Owner]. Could you connect me, please?"
3"Where are you calling from?"Say the company name only. Add more only if they ask more.Like a name tag. It says who you are. Nobody reads a brochure at the front desk.
Sample: "One Wealth Map. Is [Owner] available, please?"
4"Do you have an appointment?"Say no honestly, then redirect to availability.Like walking up without a reservation. Restaurants still seat you if you ask confidently: "No reservation, is a table free now?"
Sample: "No, I don't. It's a business matter for [Owner]. Are they available now?"
5"Does the owner know you?"Be open that this is the first time they'll connect. Then redirect to availability.Like a new neighbor knocking. You don't pretend to be a relative. Being honest keeps their trust.
Sample: "This would be our first conversation. It's regarding a business matter. Is [Owner] available?"
6"How did you get this number?"Briefly share the real source, without overexplaining. If you don't know, don't guess.Like a shop asked how it got your address. A clear answer calms people. Avoiding the question can feel suspicious.
Sample: "Our team came across your firm through our research. Is [Owner] available now?"
7"Can I tell them what it's about?"Give a short, credible reason they can pass on to the owner. Then redirect to availability.Like a sealed letter. The secretary delivers it but doesn't open it. "Business matter" says important and private.
Sample: "Sure. Please let [Owner] know it's [Your name] from One Wealth Map, calling about a business matter. Are they free now, or when should I call back?"
8They keep asking for more details.Give enough context to sound legitimate and important, without the full pitch. Then redirect.Like a movie trailer. Enough to show it's worth it. The full story is for the owner.
Sample: "We're a registered investment adviser, and it's regarding a business discussion about [Owner]'s practice. Is [Owner] available?"
9"Are you with [their firm]?" / "Are you approved by our broker-dealer?"Give a clear, honest no. Then redirect.Like being asked "Are you from the bank?" A clear "No" builds trust. A vague answer can sound like a connection that doesn't exist.
Sample: "No, we're an independent RIA, not affiliated with [firm name]. It's a business matter for [Owner]. Are they available?"
B

Owner not available

When the owner is busy, find out the best time to call back.

#SituationGoalWhy it works
10"They're with a client / in a meeting."Get a specific callback time in their time zone. Log it in GHL.Like a doctor in surgery. You ask the nurse what time they'll be out. "Later" isn't a time.
Sample: "No problem. What time do they usually finish? Would 3 or 4 PM your time work better?"
11"They're out this week."Find the day they're back.Like a store closed for the holidays. You check the reopening date instead of guessing.
Sample: "Thanks for letting me know. What day are they back? I'll call then."
12"They're busy all day, every day."Find a gap: early or late.Like catching a busy professor. Not during class. Before the first or after the last.
Sample: "Understood. Are they usually free early in the morning or near the end of the day?"
13"Everything goes through me."Make the gatekeeper your partner.Like a barangay hall. Follow their process and the secretary helps you. Fight it, and nothing moves.
Sample: "Thank you, I appreciate your help. What's the best way to get a business matter to [Owner]?"
C

Brush-offs

Say yes to their request, then ask for one more detail.

#SituationGoalWhy it works
14"If they're interested, they'll call you back."Ask for the best time to call instead.Like "we'll text you" after a job interview. You may not hear back. Setting the next step yourself helps.
Sample: "I understand. To save everyone time, when is the best time for me to call back?"
15"Send an email."Say yes, then ask for a direct email or a time.Like "leave it at the front desk." It may sit in a pile. A direct address gives it a real chance.
Sample: "Happy to. What's [Owner]'s direct email, so it reaches them directly?"
16"Send it to info@."Avoid the general inbox.Like a box marked "General." It might never get opened.
Sample: "Sure. Is there a direct email for [Owner]? If not, when is a good time to call back?"
17"Leave a message."Leave it short, and set your own follow-up.Like ordering food and waiting forever. Follow up yourself, or it may never come.
Sample: "Sure. Please let them know [Your name] from One Wealth Map called. When is a good time for me to try again?"
18"Just leave a voicemail."Keep it under 20 seconds. Name, company, a short reason, and your number twice.Like a text vs. a long letter. People finish short messages. Say your number slowly, twice.
Simple version: "Hi [Owner], this is [Your name] with One Wealth Map. I'm reaching out to a small number of firm owners about what's next for their practice. Please call me back at [number]. Again, that's [Your name], [number]."
19"We get calls like this all the time."Show respect. Keep the ask tiny.Like asking a busy cashier for one quick thing. "Two minutes" is easy to say yes to.
Sample: "I understand, and I'll keep it short. When is the best time to reach [Owner] for two minutes?"
D

When the gatekeeper decides

Stay polite and let the owner make the decision.

#SituationGoalWhy it works
20"They're not interested." (for the owner)Probe. Find out what led to this answer, without sounding confrontational.Like a helper saying "Ma'am doesn't want it." Ma'am never saw it.
Sample: "I understand. May I ask what makes you say that? That helps me know if a call with [Owner] still makes sense."
21"I handle these decisions."Find out kindly if they're an owner or staff.Like a barangay captain's secretary. They schedule, but only the captain signs.
Sample: "Thank you. Are you part of the ownership, or do you help manage operations?"
22"We already have an advisor." / "We don't need services."Acknowledge their current setup. Clarify the call isn't about replacing their advisor or selling services. Redirect to the owner.Like a tutor turned away because "the kids have a teacher." You're there for the school owner, not the kids.
Sample: "That's great that you have a setup that works. This isn't about replacing anyone or selling services. It's a business matter for [Owner]. Are they available?"
23"We're not hiring." / "We're not selling."Stay calm. Don't explain. Ask for the right person.Like a family selling their house. They don't tell the helpers first. Owner plans can be private, even from staff.
Sample: "Understood. It's a business matter for [Owner]. When is a good time to reach them?"
24"Talk to our operations manager."Be polite. See if they can help you reach the owner.Like being sent to a different window. They may not decide, but they may walk you to the right door.
Sample: "Thank you. Could they help me reach [Owner], or is there a better time to call [Owner] directly?"
E

Rules and restrictions

Follow their rules, then ask for another way to reach the owner.

#SituationGoalWhy it works
25"No unsolicited calls. Remove us."Exit politely. Mark Do Not Call in GHL right away.Like a "No Solicitors" sign. Respecting it protects the company's reputation.
Sample: "Of course. I'll make sure your number is removed. Thank you for your time."
26"Calls are recorded. They can't discuss this here."Respect it. Ask for an email or a better number.Like a bank full of CCTV. You don't argue with the rules. You ask to talk somewhere better.
Sample: "I understand. Is there a better number or email for [Owner]?"
27"Compliance has to approve this."Learn their process.Like getting a permit. Go to the right window and things move faster.
Sample: "That makes sense. Who should I send information to for review?"
28"Our broker-dealer doesn't allow these calls."Stay respectful. Never comment on their agreements.Like someone's work contract. You're not their lawyer. That's for the principals.
Sample: "Understood, I respect that. Is there another way [Owner] prefers to be contacted?"
F

Phone systems and wrong contacts

#SituationGoalWhy it works
29Phone menu (IVR)Reach a real person. Directory first, then the operator or front desk.Like a mall directory. Look up the store instead of walking every floor. Note what worked in GHL.
Tip: Search the directory by the owner's last name. If there's none, press 0 or choose the operator or front desk option.
30"No one here by that name."Ask who owns the firm now. Update GHL.Like an old address on a package. Fix it, or the next delivery fails too.
Sample: "I may have outdated information. Who owns the firm now?"
31"They retired / left the firm."Find the new owner. Update GHL.Like a restaurant with a new owner. New leadership often means new plans.
Sample: "Thank you for letting me know. Who leads the firm now?"
32"They passed away."Offer condolences and end the call. Note it in GHL.Like visiting during a wake. You show respect and leave. Never pitch.
Sample: "I'm very sorry to hear that. Thank you for telling me, and I apologize for the call."
G

Other situations

#SituationGoalWhy it works
33A chatty, friendly gatekeeperBuild rapport. Learn the owner's schedule.Like the friendly tita at the sari-sari store who tells you when the owner drops by.
Sample: "Thanks so much, [Gatekeeper]! When is usually the best time to catch [Owner]?"
34A rude gatekeeper, or they hang upStay professional. Try another day.Like a grumpy jeepney driver. Their bad day, not yours.
Sample: "Thank you for your time." Then log it and try again another day.
35"Can you hold?" (long hold)Keep the call. Get ready while waiting.Like a bank line. Fill out your form while waiting so you're ready when called.
Sample: "Sure, I'll hold. Thank you." If the hold is long: "I don't want to keep you. When is a better time to call?"
36Third callback, same gatekeeperUse their name. Mention the last call.Like the guard who now greets you by name. Being polite and consistent builds familiarity.
Sample: "Hi [Gatekeeper], it's [Your name] from One Wealth Map again. Is [Owner] available today?"
37You get cut off mid‑sentenceFinish your point calmly, then redirect.Like a jeepney stopping short of your stop. You still say "para po" and get off where you need to.
Sample: "Sorry, just to finish: it's a business matter for [Owner]. Are they available?"
38The owner answers directlySwitch to owner mode right away.Like the boss walking past you in the lobby. Don't freeze. Greet them and introduce yourself.
Sample: "Hi [Owner], this is [Your name] with One Wealth Map. We're a multi-family office, an RIA with tax, estate, insurance and back office all in-house. I'm reaching out to a small number of firm owners about what's next for their practice. Do you have two minutes?"

When you reach the owner

If the owner is still on the line, you still have a chance to ask a question.

AcknowledgeAnd mean it
→
One questionAbout their future
→
ListenFind the path
→
AskFor a specific day and time
#SituationGoalWhy it works
39"I'm good." / "We're doing well."Acknowledge with real energy. Then ask one question about what's next.Like a healthy person and insurance. Doing well today is a good time to think about later.
Sample: "That's great to hear! When you picture the practice five years from now, are you still running it, handing it off, or already out?"
40"I'm busy."Respect it. Ask for a few minutes now, or set a time.Like an elevator ride. You get one floor's worth of time. Keep it brief and get to the point.
Sample: "Sounds like I caught you at a busy time. Do you have two minutes now, or is later today better?"
41"Just send me information."Treat it as a semi-win. Try one question first, then send.Like flyers in your own inbox. Not all of them get read. Sometimes "send info" is a polite way to end the call.
Sample: "Happy to send it. So I send the right information, can I ask one quick question first?"
42"Not interested" right after your introAsk what they mean, or ask one future question.Like rejecting a package before opening it. They may not know yet what the call is about.
Sample: "I understand. May I ask what you mean? Is it the timing, or something else?"
43"What is this about?" from the ownerClear purpose in one line, then a question.Like a doctor explaining before an exam. People relax when they know why you're there.
Sample: "We're a multi-family office. I'm reaching out to a small number of firm owners about what's next for their practice, whether that's growing, partnering, or eventually stepping back. Are you the owner, or part of a larger team?"
44"Never heard of you." / "You're small."Agree, then build up the founder with approved facts.Like a new restaurant by an experienced chef. People try it because of the chef's background.
Sample: "That's fair. We're at around $300 million and growing about 40% a year, and our founder has built and sold a few firms. He's been on both sides of these conversations."
45They say yes but "I'll book it later."Offer two specific times now.Like "let's hang out sometime." Without a date, it is easy to forget.
Sample: "Let's find a time now so it's on your calendar. Would Tuesday at 10 AM or Thursday at 2 PM your time work better?"

Remember

Acknowledge first. Say it warmly so it sounds sincere. End with a question, so the conversation keeps going.
