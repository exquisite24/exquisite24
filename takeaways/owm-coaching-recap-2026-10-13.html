Coaching Recap | 10/13 Eastern Time

Mark and G-Anne · with Bea (Client Success Manager) and Arque (Success Coach)

This recap helps Mark and G-Anne get past more gatekeepers and reach more prospects. It is support, not a warning. Keep it open during calls this week.

GENERAL OBSERVATIONS

  • Both stayed polite and professional with every gatekeeper, even on wrong numbers.
  • Gatekeepers are still where most calls stop. Mark shared that he applied the 10/12 feedback but did not reach a prospect on 10/13. An advisor G-Anne reached on 10/12 said "You're too vague" and asked for an email instead.
  • The same line comes out when a call goes off script. In the call reviews, "I'm calling on behalf of Ken Himmler" came up even right after the gatekeeper said the prospect works at another office. This line was added recently at Sam's request, and it still works when the gatekeeper asks what the call is about. Bea's coaching: respond to what the gatekeeper just said first, then go back to the goal.
  • Some details were shared before anyone asked. Ken's name sometimes came up in the first answer, and AI was mentioned when "a business matter" was enough. Bea's tip: share more only when the gatekeeper asks.
  • Email came up early. On several calls, email was offered before asking for a callback time. Bea suggested treating email as the last option, unless the gatekeeper or prospect asks for it.
  • The script is being followed word for word. Both shared this during the sessions. Bea's point: the playbook is a guide. Knowing why each line is said makes it easier to say it naturally and to handle questions.

KEY TAKEAWAYS

  1. Acknowledge first. Respond to what the person said, then go back to the goal.
  2. Answer directly, then add one line. Short, direct answers sound natural and confident.
  3. Ask for a time before offering email. Gatekeepers usually won't share emails, but they may share a good time to call back.
  4. Use the playbook as a guide. Same meaning, in their own words. Facts and figures stay exactly as approved.
  5. Keep the energy up. Bea's tip: smile and use hand gestures while talking. It comes through in the voice.

WHAT'S NOT ON THEM

Some leads didn't match the office reached. In Mark's 10/13 call review, 4 of 6 calls reached a different office or a number without that contact. At one firm, the gatekeeper noticed that several contacts had been called. Bea noted there's no easy way to see in GHL if a firm was already called. Arque will follow up with Bea on this.

Next check-in: 10/14 ET coaching session, with mock gatekeeper calls and a short playbook review. Thank you both for being open today. If any line feels hard to say, post it in the group chat, and Bea and Arque can help rephrase it.

INDIVIDUAL FEEDBACK

Mark

What's working

  • Bea noted his tone already sounds professional and assertive.
  • Polite exits, even on wrong numbers.
  • In the 10/13 call review, when a gatekeeper offered help, he got the right office's number and the prospect's email.
  • In the mock call, he kept asking for a callback time and offered "morning or afternoon?"

To work on

  • When told "wrong office": "Which office is he in? Do you happen to have that number?" It shows he's listening.
  • When the prospect is not available, ask for a time first: "When's the best time to catch him today?"
  • When the greeting is unclear: "Just to confirm, is this [Company]?" So he doesn't hang up on the right firm.
  • Vary the wording from call to call so it doesn't sound memorized.

G-Anne

What's working

  • Warm, polite tone with gatekeepers.
  • In the mock call, she asked for the best callback time and followed up with "morning or afternoon?"
  • In the 10/9 call review, she spotted her own fixes: ask for a callback time and confirm the email.

To work on

  • At the front desk, keep it simple. Instead of "grow faster using AI," say:"It's about [Prospect]'s practice."
  • Skip "Do you have two minutes?" Go straight to the question while the prospect is still on the line.
  • After "not interested," ask a question that gets them thinking: "What are you focused on with your practice right now?"
  • Know the playbook tracks (sell, merge, succession, recruit) well enough to explain them without the script.
  • Tag every call in GHL and update the tracker, so recordings are easy to find for review.
