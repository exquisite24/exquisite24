Coaching takeaways: G-Anne

Based on the October 12, 2026 session and patterns from coaching and call reviews since September · 2-minute read

Key realization: The playbook is your guide, not a script to read. With gatekeepers, say less and sound sure. With prospects, ask first, then use their answer.

Stop

  • Pitching to the gatekeeper. (10/12)No AI, leads or services. Save it for the owner.
  • Second-guessing yourself when a gatekeeper presses. (9/24, 10/12)Breathe, then go back to your one line.
  • Stopping at "No, she's not expecting my call." (10/12)Add the reason, then make your request.
  • Repeating "business matter." (10/12)
  • Pitching right after "I'm not interested." (10/12)Ask an open question instead.
  • Phrases that give an easy no: (10/12)"if you're interested," "would you be open to hearing about," "can I ask you a quick question?"
  • Old playbook questions, like "What's the biggest thing holding back your growth?" (10/12)

Continue

  • Your clear, direct intro. (9/23)
  • Your warm tone. (9/24, 10/12)
  • Clean, compliant calls and voicemails. Client call reviews found no issues on your live calls. (9/24 to 10/5)
  • Asking questions while they're still on the line. (10/12)
  • Going for a specific day when they say "next week." (10/12)

Start

  • First name only, then a polite request:"Hi, this is Gladys from One Wealth Map, calling for [Owner]. Could you put me through, please? Thank you."
  • Say "our founder, Ken Himmler," never just "Ken." Never say "Ken asked me to connect with you."Client call reviews flag that line.
  • Ask the v2 routing question before you pitch.Like a good doctor: ask first, then give the right medicine.
  • Get their direct number the first time they say "I have to go." (10/12)
  • Steady pace, with emphasis on each point. (10/12)The first 20 seconds set the tone.

Lines to keep on your screen

Gatekeeper, "What's this regarding?" "It's about [Owner]'s advisory practice and some longer-term business plans. I'm calling on behalf of our founder, Ken Himmler, and it's best discussed with [Owner] directly. Could you put me through, please? Thank you."

Gatekeeper, "Is she expecting your call?" "No, she isn't expecting my call. I'm calling on behalf of our founder, Ken Himmler. It's about her practice. Could you put me through, please? Thank you."

Prospect, "Not interested." "I understand. What are you focused on right now with your practice? Keeping what you've built, or growing it?"

Prospect, "I have to go." "Of course. Before you go, what's the best number to reach you directly?"

Every answer: answer their question, add one line of context, then end with a request or open question. Only say what's true, and only use approved facts. Dates in gray show when the pattern came up.
