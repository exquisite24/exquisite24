# Gatekeeper Objections: Practice List

**For:** Mark and G-Anne | **Campaign:** RIA Outreach

**Rule for every call:** never hang up with nothing. Leave with a **transfer**, a **callback time**, a **direct line**, or an **email**.

**Your mindset:** the gatekeeper protects the owner's time. Be polite, short and confident. You are not selling to them. You are asking for two minutes with their boss.

---

## A. Screening questions

**1. "What's this regarding?"**
- **Goal:** Give a short, clear reason and ask for the owner again.
- **Coaching point:** One sentence only. Long answers sound like sales.

**2. "Is this a sales call?"**
- **Goal:** Answer honestly, then give the real reason.
- **Coaching point:** "No" alone sounds suspicious. Always follow it with why you're calling.

**3. "What company are you with? What do you do?"**
- **Goal:** Explain One Wealth Map in one line, then go back to the owner.
- **Coaching point:** The gatekeeper doesn't need the full pitch, just enough to pass you along.

**4. "Does he know you? Is he expecting your call?"**
- **Goal:** Be honest without losing confidence.
- **Coaching point:** Never pretend to know the owner. Honesty protects trust, and you can still sound calm and direct.

**5. "How did you get this number?"**
- **Goal:** Give the real source and move on.
- **Coaching point:** Be specific and honest. Confirm the approved wording with Sam.

**6. "Can I tell him what it's about?"**
- **Goal:** Share enough to be passed along and keep the details for the owner.
- **Coaching point:** "Confidential business matter" signals importance without pitching.

---

## B. Owner not available

**7. "He's with a client."**
- **Goal:** Get a specific callback time.
- **Coaching point:** "Later" isn't a time. Offer two options, then log it in GHL.

**8. "He's out of the office this week."**
- **Goal:** Find out when he's back and set a date to call.
- **Coaching point:** Ask about the best day and time, not just "next week."

**9. "He's in meetings all day, every day."**
- **Goal:** Find the gaps in his day.
- **Coaching point:** Ask when he usually starts or ends his day. Owners are often free early or late.

**10. "He doesn't take calls. Everything goes through me."**
- **Goal:** Treat the gatekeeper as your partner, not your obstacle.
- **Coaching point:** Ask how they usually get important matters to the owner, and follow that process.

---

## C. Brush-offs

**11. "Send an email."**
- **Goal:** Get the owner's direct email, or at least a time to call.
- **Coaching point:** Accept the request, but always ask for one more thing.

**12. "Send it to info@ and we'll pass it along."**
- **Goal:** Avoid the general inbox.
- **Coaching point:** Ask politely for a direct address "so it doesn't get lost." If they say no, ask for a time instead.

**13. "Leave a message and he'll call you back."**
- **Goal:** Leave a short message and still set your own follow-up.
- **Coaching point:** Don't wait for a callback. Ask when you should try again.

**14. "Just leave a voicemail."**
- **Goal:** Leave a clear voicemail under 20 seconds.
- **Coaching point:** Say your name and number slowly, twice. Time yourself.

**15. "We get calls like this all the time."**
- **Goal:** Show you respect their time and stand out.
- **Coaching point:** Agree with them, then keep your request very small: two minutes.

---

## D. The gatekeeper decides

**16. "He's not interested."** (said on the owner's behalf)
- **Goal:** Let the owner decide for himself.
- **Coaching point:** Don't argue. Ask if you can send one short note.

**17. "I handle these decisions."**
- **Goal:** Find out if they're an owner or staff.
- **Coaching point:** Ask kindly about their role. Ownership questions need the owner.

**18. "We already have an advisor / we're not looking for services."**
- **Goal:** Clear up the confusion. You're not offering them financial services.
- **Coaching point:** They may think you're selling to the firm's clients. Explain it's an owner to owner conversation.

**19. "We're not hiring / he's not looking for a job."**
- **Goal:** Clarify without arguing.
- **Coaching point:** The conversation is about the practice's plans. Stay calm and ask for the right person.

**20. "We're not selling the firm."**
- **Goal:** Protect confidentiality and keep the door open.
- **Coaching point:** Never discuss selling with the gatekeeper. The owner's plans may be private, even from staff.

---

## E. Rules and restrictions

**21. "We don't accept unsolicited calls. Please remove us."**
- **Goal:** Exit politely and mark Do Not Call in GHL.
- **Coaching point:** This is a win if handled well. Respecting it protects One Wealth Map's reputation.

**22. "All our calls are recorded. He can't discuss outside business on this line."**
- **Goal:** Respect the rule and find another way to reach him.
- **Coaching point:** Don't fight compliance. Ask for the best email or a better number.

**23. "Our compliance department has to approve contact like this."**
- **Goal:** Find out the process.
- **Coaching point:** Ask who to send information to. Following the process shows professionalism.

**24. "We're with [big broker-dealer]. He can't take these calls."**
- **Goal:** Stay respectful and ask for another channel.
- **Coaching point:** Never comment on their agreements. That's for the principals.

---

## F. Phone systems and wrong contacts

**25. Phone menu (IVR) with no clear option**
- **Goal:** Reach a real person.
- **Coaching point:** Try the directory by last name first. Then "new clients," since a person usually answers fast. Note which option worked in GHL.

**26. "There's no one here by that name."**
- **Goal:** Find out if the contact moved or the data is wrong.
- **Coaching point:** Ask who owns the firm now. Update GHL so no one calls the wrong person again.

**27. "He retired / he left the firm."**
- **Goal:** Find the new owner, and maybe where the old contact went.
- **Coaching point:** This can be a lead. A new owner or a recent change can mean the firm is going through a transition.

**28. "He passed away."**
- **Goal:** Respond with kindness and end the call.
- **Coaching point:** Offer condolences. Don't pitch. Note it in GHL so no one calls again.

---

## G. Outside the box

**29. The gatekeeper is chatty and friendly.**
- **Goal:** Build rapport and learn useful details.
- **Coaching point:** Ask their name and use it. Ask about the owner's schedule. A friendly gatekeeper is your best ally.

**30. The gatekeeper is rude or hangs up mid-sentence.**
- **Goal:** Stay professional and try again another day.
- **Coaching point:** It's not personal. Log it, then try a different time. A different person may answer.

**31. "Can you hold?" (long hold)**
- **Goal:** Use the time well and not lose the call.
- **Coaching point:** Prepare your opening while waiting. If the hold is too long, ask for a callback time.

**32. The gatekeeper offers someone else ("Talk to our operations manager").**
- **Goal:** Decide whether that person can help.
- **Coaching point:** Be polite. Ownership plans need the owner, but this person may help you reach him.

**33. The gatekeeper asks you to explain in detail.**
- **Goal:** Keep it short without sounding secretive.
- **Coaching point:** Share one line. Save the details for the owner, out of respect for his privacy.

**34. You call back for the third time and the same gatekeeper answers.**
- **Goal:** Turn repetition into familiarity.
- **Coaching point:** Use their name, mention the last call, and stay friendly. Persistence plus politeness builds trust.

**35. The owner answers his own phone unexpectedly.**
- **Goal:** Switch from gatekeeper mode to owner mode right away.
- **Coaching point:** Don't freeze. Confirm his name, then go straight to your opening.

---

## Quick reminders

- **Use first names:** the owner's and the gatekeeper's.
- **Be short:** your reason fits in one sentence.
- **Be honest:** never pretend to know the owner or make up a reason.
- **Always ask for one more thing:** a time, a direct line, or an email.
- **Log everything in GHL:** gatekeeper's name, best time, what worked.
- **Respect "remove us":** a polite exit is a professional win.
