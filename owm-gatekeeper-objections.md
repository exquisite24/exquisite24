# Gatekeeper Objections: Practice List

**For:** Mark and G-Anne | **Campaign:** RIA Outreach (Playbook v2)

**Rule for every call:** never hang up with nothing. Leave with a **transfer**, a **callback time**, a **direct line**, or an **email**.

**Your mindset:** the gatekeeper is like a building guard. Their job is not to stop everyone. It's to let in people with a clear reason. Be polite, short and confident. You're asking for two minutes with the owner.

**Before you dial (Playbook Section 3):** check GHL and the lead source. Know if you're calling a **firm owner** or an **individual advisor**, and the person's first name.

**Never say to a gatekeeper (Playbook Section 1):** anything about selling, buying, merging or recruiting, any numbers, or who else we're talking to. Every owner conversation is confidential.

**How to read this:** each row has the situation, your goal, and **why it works**, explained with an everyday example.

---

## A. Screening questions

| # | Situation | Goal | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "What's this regarding?" | Give a one-sentence reason, then ask for the owner again. | **Like telling a guard "I'm here to see Mr. Reyes."** You don't show them your whole presentation. Short answers sound like you belong. Long ones sound like a sales pitch. |
| 2 | "Is this a sales call?" | Answer honestly, then give the real reason. | **Like a guard asking "Delivery?"** If you only say "No," they get suspicious. If you say "No, I'm here for a business meeting," they relax. A clear reason replaces doubt. |
| 3 | "What company are you with? What do you do?" | Say who One Wealth Map is in one line (playbook line). | **Like a calling card.** One line tells them who you are. Nobody reads a brochure at the front desk. |
| 4 | "Does the owner know you? Are they expecting your call?" | Be honest and stay confident. | **Like a new neighbor knocking on a door.** You don't pretend to be a relative. You say who you are, kindly. If you lie and get caught, the door closes for good. |
| 5 | "How did you get this number?" | Give the real source. Confirm the wording with Sam. | **Like a customer asking a shop how it got their address.** A clear, honest answer calms them. Dodging makes it feel shady. |
| 6 | "Can I tell them what it's about?" | Share enough to be passed along. Save the details. | **Like sealing a letter.** The secretary delivers it but doesn't read it. "Confidential business matter" tells them it's important and private. |

## B. Owner not available

| # | Situation | Goal | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | "They're with a client." | Get a specific callback time and log it in GHL. | **Like a doctor in surgery.** You don't just leave. You ask the nurse, "What time will the doctor be out?" "Later" is not a time, and without one you start from zero. |
| 8 | "They're out of the office this week." | Find out the day they're back. | **Like a store closed for the holidays.** You check the sign for the reopening date so you come back on the right day, not by guessing. |
| 9 | "They're in meetings all day, every day." | Find a gap in their day. | **Like catching a busy professor.** You don't knock during class. You come before the first class or after the last one. Owners are often free early or late. |
| 10 | "They don't take calls. Everything goes through me." | Make the gatekeeper your partner. | **Like a barangay hall.** You follow their process, and the secretary helps you. Fight the process, and nothing moves. |

## C. Brush-offs

| # | Situation | Goal | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | "Send an email." | Say yes, then ask for the owner's direct email or a time. | **Like "just leave it at the front desk."** Your letter may sit in a pile. Asking to address it directly gives it a real chance to be read. |
| 12 | "Send it to info@ and we'll pass it along." | Avoid the general inbox. | **Like dropping a letter in a box marked "General."** It might take weeks, or never get read. A direct address is like handing it to the person. |
| 13 | "Leave a message and they'll call you back." | Leave a short message and set your own follow-up. | **Like ordering food and waiting forever.** If you don't follow up, it may never come. Owning the next step keeps you in control. |
| 14 | "Just leave a voicemail." | Use the playbook voicemail. Keep it under 20 seconds. | **Like a text message vs. a long letter.** People finish short messages. Say your number slowly, twice, like reading a phone number to a friend. |
| 15 | "We get calls like this all the time." | Show respect and keep the ask small. | **Like asking a busy cashier for one quick thing.** "Just two minutes" is easy to say yes to. A long request is easy to say no to. |

## D. The gatekeeper decides

| # | Situation | Goal | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 16 | "They're not interested." (said for the owner) | Let the owner decide. Ask to send one short note. | **Like a house helper saying "Ma'am doesn't want it."** Ma'am never actually saw it. A short note lets the right person decide, without arguing with the helper. |
| 17 | "I handle these decisions." | Find out kindly if they're an owner or staff. | **Like the secretary of a barangay captain.** They can schedule visits, but only the captain can sign. Respect the secretary, but some matters need the boss. |
| 18 | "We already have an advisor." / "We don't need services." | Clear up the confusion. | **Like a tutor being turned away because "the kids already have a teacher."** You're not there for the kids. You want to talk to the school owner. Explain it's for the owner, from our founder. |
| 19 | "We're not hiring." / "They're not looking for a job." | Stay calm. Don't explain the offer. Ask for the right person. | **Like an envelope marked "personal."** You don't open it at the reception desk. The details are for the person it's addressed to. |
| 20 | "We're not selling the firm." | Protect confidentiality. Never discuss selling with staff. | **Like a family deciding to sell their house.** They don't tell the helpers first. The owner's plans may be private, even from their own team. |

## E. Rules and restrictions

| # | Situation | Goal | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 21 | "We don't accept unsolicited calls. Remove us." | Exit politely and mark Do Not Call in GHL. | **Like a gate with a "No Solicitors" sign.** If you knock anyway, the whole village remembers your company badly. Walking away politely protects One Wealth Map's name. |
| 22 | "Calls are recorded. They can't discuss outside business on this line." | Respect the rule. Ask for an email or a better number. | **Like a bank with CCTV everywhere.** You don't argue with the bank's rules. You ask to talk somewhere more suitable. |
| 23 | "Our compliance team has to approve this." | Learn their process. Ask where to send information. | **Like getting a building permit.** You don't skip the office. You go through the right window, and things move faster. |
| 24 | "We're with a large broker-dealer. They can't take these calls." | Stay respectful. Ask for another channel. Never comment on their agreements. | **Like an employee under contract.** You're not their lawyer, so you don't give advice about their contract. That's for the principals. |

## F. Phone systems and wrong contacts

| # | Situation | Goal | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | Phone menu (IVR) with no clear option | Reach a real person. Try the directory first, then "new clients." Note what worked in GHL. | **Like a mall directory.** You don't walk every floor. You look up the store. "New clients" works like the main entrance: someone is always there. |
| 26 | "There's no one here by that name." | Ask who owns the firm now. Update GHL. | **Like an old address on a package.** If you don't fix it, the next delivery goes to the wrong house again. |
| 27 | "They retired." / "They left the firm." | Find the new owner. Update GHL. | **Like a restaurant with a new owner.** A change in leadership often means change in plans. That can be an opening. |
| 28 | "They passed away." | Offer condolences and end the call. Note it in GHL. | **Like visiting a home during a wake.** You show respect and leave. Selling at that moment is never okay. |

## G. Outside the box

| # | Situation | Goal | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 29 | The gatekeeper is chatty and friendly. | Build rapport and learn the owner's schedule. | **Like the friendly tita at the sari-sari store** who tells you when the owner usually drops by. Be friendly back. They're your best source of information. |
| 30 | The gatekeeper is rude or hangs up. | Stay professional and try another day. | **Like a grumpy jeepney driver.** It's their bad day, not yours. Next time, a different driver, or a better mood. |
| 31 | "Can you hold?" (long hold) | Keep the call. Prepare while waiting. | **Like waiting in line at a bank.** You fill out your form while waiting, so you're ready when your number is called. If the line is too long, ask for another time. |
| 32 | "Talk to our operations manager instead." | Decide if that person can help you reach the owner. | **Like being sent to a different office window.** Be polite. They may not decide, but they may walk you to the right door. |
| 33 | The gatekeeper wants all the details. | Stay short without sounding secretive. | **Like a movie trailer.** You share enough to show it's worth it, and save the full story for the owner. |
| 34 | Third callback, same gatekeeper | Use their name and mention the last call. | **Like the guard at your office who now greets you by name.** Polite persistence turns a stranger into an ally. |
| 35 | The owner answers unexpectedly. | Switch to owner mode right away. Use the playbook opening. | **Like waiting in a lobby and the boss walks past you.** Don't freeze. Greet them and introduce yourself. Always have your opening ready. |

---

## Quick reminders

- **Use first names:** the owner's and the gatekeeper's.
- **Be short:** your reason fits in one sentence.
- **Be honest:** never pretend to know the owner or make up a reason.
- **Keep it confidential:** no deal talk, no numbers, no other firm names.
- **Always ask for one more thing:** a time, a direct line, or an email.
- **Log everything in GHL:** gatekeeper's name, best time, what worked.
- **Respect "remove us":** a polite exit is a professional win.
